# What the record keeps about failure

A command fails. The agent moves on, and the failure dissolves into the
transcript: what was run, what it printed, and what was tried next —
flattened into prose. Weeks later the same failure returns and nobody can
say whether it was seen before, what fixed it, or whether anything fixed it.

---

## Failures as entries, not prose

OpenState records failed attempts the way it records everything else: the
command, the outcome, the session, the work it touched. A later session can
ask what failed around a piece of work and get the recorded attempts instead
of a summary written from memory.

The record does not diagnose. It keeps the attempts where they can be found,
beside the decisions and edits they belong to.

---

## Errors stay visible

A separate legacy corpus of 21,372 OpenState tool calls holds 19 calls whose
tool status is error: 0.09%. The errors are entries in the corpus like any
other call, which is the point: recorded failures can be counted, and
unrecorded ones cannot be.

Reduction in repeated failures is not measured at the session level. The claim
here is narrower: the attempts are kept.

---

## What keeping failures cannot do

A shelf of past failures does not stop the next one. It shortens the next
diagnosis, because the last one is written down. Sources and method:
[measured evidence](../../docs/evidence.md).
